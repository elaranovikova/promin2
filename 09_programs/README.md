# 09_programs: the reconstructed programs

Generated by `06_scripts/reconstruct_programs.py` from the extraction of all 85
scans. One text file per deck with the numbered instruction band, plus
`overview.csv` and `overview.json` with the figures.

**27 decks · 222 cards · 2138 instructions.** Of the 222 cards, 204 differ in
content; the remaining 18 are duplicates.

## How the order comes about

A deck is one folder of the scan archive. Every card carries 10 instructions,
and the cards fill the instruction memory (cells 000-159) in order. That order
follows the scan order and the position of the cards on the sheet (top ->
bottom).

This assumption is tested, not taken on faith: jump instructions name
instruction numbers, and those have to fall inside their own deck.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Jump targets inside instruction memory 000-159 | **247 of 247 = 100 %** |
| Jump targets inside their own deck | 216 of 247 = 87.4 % |

Only the direct jumps (БП, УП1, УП2) are counted. For `БПII` the address part
does not name the instruction number but the cell holding the target, and that
value is not on the card and cannot be checked here.

The decks where it does *not* work out are exactly those whose folder name
announces gaps: `АУ_нет_3-5` ("without 3-5"), `3_1-11-15`,
`Рассчёт_себестоимости_(7)` (4 of 7 cards), along with `Прочее`
("miscellaneous"), which is not a deck at all but a box of leftovers. For
`АУ_нет_3-5` the hit rate rises to 100 % as soon as the three missing cards are
counted as a gap; the deck is then 10 cards long, exactly like the complete
`!_полная_АУ`.

## The sharpest cross-check

Of the program "АУ" the archive holds **three physically different stacks**,
scanned separately: `!_полная_АУ` (10 cards), `АУ_1-8` (8) and `АУ_нет_3-5`
(7). If the reconstruction is right, the corresponding cards have to agree.

**249 of 250 compared instructions are identical.** The one deviation
(`Чт 003` against `Чт 069`) sits on the card with the worst grid fit in the
deck (17.0 px against 8.4 px for its counterpart); inspecting the scan by eye
sides with the two decks that agree. That puts the error rate of the pipeline
at roughly **1 misreading per 250 instructions**, and the quality measure
`grid_deviation_px` had flagged the card in question in advance.

## What the programs do

They are computing programs for an engineering calculator, written for a
single-address machine with one accumulator: read a value from a cell, compute
with it, write it back, branch. Reading and writing together make up almost
half of all instructions.

The deck names come from the captions on the cards and name the task: network
planning (`Сеть`), cost accounting, service life of equipment, multifactor
analysis, root mean square deviation, ASU (automated management system). So
mostly business and statistical computing tasks.

The longest programs fill the instruction memory almost completely: the
multifactor analysis 150 instructions, `2_1-15(16)` 149, `2_1-14` and `5_1-14`
139 each, out of 160 available cells.

Elementary functions appear sparingly and fit the task at hand: square root and
arc cosine in the statistical decks, exponential and logarithm in the
depreciation and growth calculations, sine/cosine/tangent only in the АУ
program.

## One open point: code 19

Table 5 of the textbook covers the Промінь-**М** and lists code 19 as the
hyperbolic sine. For the Промінь-**2**, table 6 names an extra instruction СчП
(readdressing and loop counting), but the code column of that row is empty in
the book.

The way the cards use it argues that on the Промінь-2 code 19 is that extra
instruction: of 44 occurrences, 20 are followed immediately by a conditional
jump (12 of them backwards, so a loop) and 15 by another 19, in chains of up
to four. A trigonometric function followed in 45 % of cases by "jump if S = 0"
makes no sense; a hyperbolic sine in a cost accounting program even less.

This is a reasoned guess, not proof. A circuit diagram or an operating manual
for the Промінь-2 would settle it.

## Columns of `overview.csv`

`cards`, `instructions`, `empty` (unused slots), `jumps_direct` (БП, УП1, УП2),
`jumps_indirect` (ЧтII, ЗпII, БПII), `jumps_within_deck`, `backward_jumps`
(loops), `hit_rate_within_deck` (the check rate), `highest_jump_target`,
`halts`, `cell_accesses`, `constant_accesses` (cells 080-099 and 180-199),
`distinct_cells`, `highest_cell`.

## The most demanding program

`Сеть_(25)`: network planning. It is the only deck that uses all three
instructions with second-rank addresses together (16 accesses): `ЧтII` and
`ЗпII` read and write indirectly, `БПII` is a *computed* jump. Three cells
(016, 021, 050) serve as pointers and are advanced by `СчП`, so the program
walks over arrays instead of using fixed addresses. On top of that, eight
backward jumps spanning up to 102 instructions, and 16 print points.

Of its 25 cards only 13 are in the archive, so a good half of the program is
lost.
